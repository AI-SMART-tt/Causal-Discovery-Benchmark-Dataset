Clarifying the contribution and its pathways to practical applications.

We sincerely thank the reviewer for encouraging us to make the connection between causal discovery and transportation decision-making more explicit. We appreciate that identifying a graph is only one step towards a useful transportation application, and that the subsequent use of the discovered relationships deserves clearer explanation. First, the principal contribution of the present study is to identify candidate relationship structures that can inform behavioural model specification and the design of subsequent intervention studies. Their potential value lies in helping researchers distinguish competing explanations and examine which relationships warrant further investigation. As the present analysis does not directly evaluate the benefits of specific pricing or dispatch policies, we frame its policy implications as directions for further testing rather than as demonstrated policy outcomes. Second, as an initial step towards connecting structure discovery with an actionable transportation question, we conducted a preliminary downstream modelling analysis to examine whether the relationships identified by FT-LCD can help specify charging-behaviour models. 

The analysis considered two outcomes: charging-time category (a classification task) and charging-initiation SOC (a regression task). For each task, we compared five input specifications: all eligible variables, domain-prespecified variables, SHAP-selected variables, parent sets identified by causal discovery without LLM-derived structural preferences, and parent sets identified by FT-LCD. We evaluated these alternatives using multiple model families, including multinomial logistic regression, random forests, and LightGBM, with ridge regression additionally used for the SOC prediction task. Hyperparameter selection followed a consistent search budget across alternative input specifications within each task and model family. The models were evaluated on later-date observations using task-appropriate metrics, including multiclass log-loss for charging-time classification and mean absolute error (MAE) for SOC prediction. We also used paired vehicle-level bootstrap comparisons to assess uncertainty in the performance differences. Importantly, the experiment assesses whether FT-LCD-derived parent sets provide useful inputs for downstream behavioural modelling; it does not establish the correctness of the complete causal graph or demonstrate the effects of specific transportation interventions. We believe this analysis provides an initial empirical link between causal structure discovery and the practical specification of electric-vehicle charging-behaviour models. 

The results provide preliminary empirical evidence that FT-LCD can support downstream charging-behaviour modelling. As shown in Fig. S5 and Fig. S6, FT-LCD-derived parent sets achieved the lowest Log-loss for charging-time classification and the lowest MAE for charging-initiation SOC across all three model families. Compared with all-eligible-variables baseline, the relative reductions in Log-loss ranged from 0.22% to 0.77%, while the relative reductions in MAE ranged from 0.78% to 1.72%. These findings suggest that the relationships identified by FT-LCD can provide a useful basis for selecting model inputs, rather than relying solely on conventional variable selection or causal discovery without LLM-derived structural preferences. This preliminary analysis addresses the reviewer's concern about the practical relevance of the discovered structures by demonstrating their potential value for behavioural model specification. However, evaluating the actual effects of specific transportation interventions requires additional data and identification conditions, such as records of implemented interventions, reliable variation in policy exposure, and appropriate counterfactual comparisons. The available observational charging-event data do not provide a sufficient basis for credibly estimating the causal effects of specific pricing, scheduling, or dispatch policies. We therefore regard the downstream modelling results as an initial step towards practical application, while identifying policy-effect evaluation as an important direction for future research.
